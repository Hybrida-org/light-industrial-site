The problem it fixes

The number you see is the number on the shelf.

Most workshops run on three things that do not talk to each other: a spreadsheet for stock, a quoting template, and whatever the accountant uses. Nobody knows what is on the shelf until someone walks to the shelf. Quotes get built from last year’s prices. Jobs go out late because a part was “in stock” and was not.

The ERP module puts stock, purchasing, quoting, jobs and deliveries on one screen, so the number you see is the number on the shelf.

What it does

The whole job, end to end.

Stock

  • One record per item, with your own codes and descriptions.
  • Buy in one unit, use in another. Sheets to metres, boxes to pieces, drums to litres. Conversions are set once per item.
  • Every movement is logged: received, issued to a job, adjusted, returned. You can always see why a count changed.
  • Minimum levels and reorder suggestions, so you order before you run out, not after.
  • Categories, locations and bins for shops with more than one store.

Suppliers and purchase orders

  • A price list per supplier per item, with the history of every price you have paid.
  • Raise a purchase order in a minute, PDF it, email it to the supplier from inside the system.
  • Receive against the order. Partial deliveries are normal, so the system tracks what is still outstanding.
  • Price-jump alerts when a supplier’s price moves more than you would expect.

Quotes

  • Build a quote from your stock items and your products, with your margins.
  • Group lines into products so the customer sees one line and you see the full parts list.
  • Approve, PDF, email. Convert an approved quote into a job or a delivery note with one click.

Job cards

  • Open a job from a quote. Issue parts to it. Track labour and hours.
  • See what each job actually cost against what you quoted it at.
  • Know which jobs are waiting on parts before the customer phones.

Delivery notes and customers

  • Delivery notes come off the quote, so nothing is retyped. Several deliveries per job if that is how it ships.
  • Signature on delivery, PDF filed against the customer.
  • Customer records with their quotes, jobs, deliveries and outstanding items in one view.

Products and bills of material

  • Define the things you sell as a list of the things you buy. Change a component price and every quote that uses it updates.

Accounting

  • Customers, suppliers and invoices sync to Xero, QuickBooks or Sage. Your accountant keeps their system. You stop capturing twice.

Reports

  • Stock value, slow movers, supplier spend, quote win rate, job margin. Export anything to Excel.

An assistant for every role

It suggests; you approve.

An AI layer sits on top of the whole system. It reads your data, it knows your suppliers and customers, and it answers in plain language. Each person gets the help their job needs.

  • Owner or manager

    Ask a question, get an answer. “What did we spend with our top three suppliers last quarter?” “Which jobs are running over quote?” “What is sitting on the shelf that has not moved in six months?” No report builder, no export.

  • Sales

    Paste a customer’s email or WhatsApp and get a draft quote with the right items and your margins. It flags parts that are out of stock before you send it, and reminds you which quotes have gone quiet.

  • Storeman

    Photograph the delivery note and the goods are booked. It warns when a delivery is short, when a supplier’s part code does not match yours, or when a count looks wrong against the last one.

  • Admin

    It spots duplicate items, missing prices and suppliers with two names, and offers to clean them up. Every correction you make, it remembers.

It never changes a record without a person saying yes. It suggests; you approve.
