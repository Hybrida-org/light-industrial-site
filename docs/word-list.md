# The words these trades actually use

Draft 1 — 2026-09-22. For HYBRIDA-893.

Two columns matter: **what our site says today**, and **what a person in a South
African stores room or workshop says**. Where they differ, theirs wins.

Nothing has been changed on the site yet. Mark this up first.

---

## 1. The one real finding

**In South Africa it is a GRV, not a GRN.**

The ticket assumed GRN (goods received note). That is the British and Indian
term. Here the standard word is **GRV — goods received voucher** — because
Pastel, Sage Evolution, Omni Accounts and every local package built on top of
them call the screen a GRV. Almost everyone in this market has touched Pastel,
so the word came off the screen and onto the floor.

It is used as a verb too: *"has it been GRV'd?"*, *"do the GRV"*.

We currently say "capture the receipt". Nobody says that. They say **GRV it**,
**book it in**, or **receive it**.

This is the single highest-value swap on the page, and it is also a trust
signal: a stranger who says GRV has been in a stores room.

## 3. Stock or inventory?

**Use "stock" in the copy. Keep "inventory" only where we are chasing search
traffic.**

Every South African vendor hedges by using both in one breath — Omni Accounts
titles its page *"Inventory Management Software | Stock Control"*, Sage ZA sells
*"stock management"* under an *"inventory"* URL. That hedging tells you the
split: *inventory* is the word people type into Google (it is what the global
software industry indexes on), *stock* is the word they say out loud.

So: **stock** in headlines and body copy, **inventory** allowed in the page
title, meta description and ad keywords.

Same rule for **GRV**: say it in the copy, but do not build an ad keyword on it
— people search for a problem, not for their own jargon.

## 4. By trade

**Fabricators and steel shops.** Buy against a **cutting list**. Off-cuts and
drops are stock. They **raise a PO** on a merchant, get a **waybill** or
delivery note with the load, and sign a **POD** (proof of delivery). Our
"Fabricators" card is close; it says "supplier quote into a job", which is right.

**HVAC and aircon installers.** Everything hangs off a **job card** — confirmed
live usage, local suppliers publish "installer completion job cards". Parts are
**booked out to a job** (or "issued to the job"), not "allocated". When parts
land they are **booked in** first, then booked out. This is a whole vocabulary
we do not use on the site at all, because we do not talk about jobs yet.

**Tile shops and builders' merchants.** Split between **delivery** and
**collection** — SA tile retailers list both as separate flows. A customer
collecting gets a **collection slip** or **picking slip**; a customer being
delivered gets a **delivery note** and signs a **POD**. Trade buyers are served
at the **trade counter**; we already say "counter sales", which is right.

**Wholesalers.** The most Pastel-shaped of the four. GRV, **stock take**,
**stock on hand**, **short delivered**, **claim**, **RTS** (return to supplier),
**credit note**. Three-way match between PO, GRV and supplier invoice is a
named, everyday job here — but call it "matching", not "three-way reconcile".

## 5. Words to never put on the page

Corporate software labels. None of these are said out loud by anyone who
touches stock:

procure-to-pay · order-to-cash · source-to-settle · spend management ·
supply chain visibility · digital transformation · normalisation ·
data ingestion · OCR · anomaly detection · SKU velocity · three-way reconcile

"SKU" is a borderline case — wholesalers do say it, fabricators do not. Avoid
it in shared copy.

## 7. If you want one change today

Change **"Capture the receipt"** to **"GRV it"** in the six-step flow, and
**"Capture"** to **"Receive"** in the hero diagram. It is two words, it costs
nothing, and it is the sentence that most makes us sound like we have stood in
a stores room.
